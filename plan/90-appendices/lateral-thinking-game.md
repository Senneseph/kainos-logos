# Lateral thinking exercises.

I think this would be a good example to help test the robustness of our reasoning ability. We could do other lateral thinking challenges as well. But the interesting thing would be if we can demonstrate: from training data, when presented with terms it may not know fully, can it recall and apply this pattern to solve it without understanding exactly what a Swede is other than it is a "Nationality", etc. Maybe sometimes it doesn't know a term like "smokes" at all and must infer its application in this context. Maybe sometimes the puzzle is broken or clues are missing. If it can come back and say "I need x piece of information" or present a logical contradiction as to why the puzzle is wrong, etc, that also would be a success. And to take it even further, we could have it tested with n-dimensional facts. We would hope that we are able to pick up the dimensions required with the prompt and then to apply the pattern generically to the data in that fasion.

# Puzzle

Here’s the setup: There are five houses, each painted a different color. In each house lives a person with a different nationality. These five owners drink a certain type of beverage, smoke a certain brand of cigar, and keep a certain pet. No owners have the same pet, smoke the same cigar, or drink the same beverage.

The question is, who owns the fish?

The person who smokes Pall Mall rears birds
The owner of the yellow house smokes Dunhill
The man living in the center house drinks milk
The Norwegian lives in the first house
The man who smokes blends lives next to the one who keeps cats
The man who keeps horses lives next to the man who smokes Dunhill
The owner who smokes BlueMaster drinks beer
