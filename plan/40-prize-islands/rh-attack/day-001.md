## 3. The 1/2 question — answered in five layers

This is where the owner's "what do we need about 1/2 for primes" finally
resolves into concrete, *named* objects rather than vibes.

1. **Arithmetic (halving / 2-adic).** Every odd prime
   `p = (p−1)/2 + (p+1)/2`; the "1/2" is exactly the remainder-1 of dividing p
   by 2. The *splitting depth* `v₂(p−1)` (how many even halvings p−1 admits)
   EXHAUSTS after d steps — "coming down to 1/2 and stopping" is this
   2-adic ladder running out. Census to 2e6: max depth 18; histogram
   {1:74516, 2:37300, 3:18519, …}.
2. **The two-squares key (the owner's own example).** Every odd prime is a
   *difference* of two squares, uniquely adjacent (7=2²−1²). Whether it is a
   *sum* of two squares is decided **entirely by p mod 4**, i.e. by how many
   halves fit into (p−1)/2. Verified to x≤3000: **211 primes ≡1 (mod 4) each
   have exactly one sum-of-two-squares representation (0 misses); 218 primes
   ≡3 (mod 4) are sums of two squares for 0 of them (0 counterexamples — must
   be 0).** `1/2 is the key; 4 is 1/2 squared.`
3. **Balance, not location (Chebyshev bias).** 1/2 is where the two prime
   colors (1 vs 3 mod 4) *balance in the mean* (both ~ x/2ln x), yet the 3s
   **lead at 99.83% of x** (log-measure 99.96%; Rubinstein–Sarnak asymptote
   ~99.15%). Final counts 74416 vs 74516 — equal in the limit, lopsided
   everywhere. *"1/2 is where the mass balances, not where it is."*
4. **The critical line (duplicate/isometry).** Under the functional-equation
   mirror `n↦n↦ 1/s`, the mirror of the arrow `n^{−s}` is `n^{−(1−s)}` and the
   length ratio is `n^{2σ−1}`. That ratio is `n^0=1` for **every** n **only
   at σ=1/2** (measured in S3d). The line σ=1/2 is the unique place where
   "duplicating" an arrow is **isometry-invariant** — the geometric reason a
   standing wave can be exact there and nowhere else. This is the real,
   checkable kernel behind "1/2 = the geometric center."
5. **Why the zeros can be on the line (leak test, S3c).** If you *break the
   mirror* — lift the primitive conductor-4 L-function β(s) to the
   non-primitive conductor 12 as `L₁₂(s)=(1+2^{−s})β(s)` — a zero appears
   **off the line** at `s = iπ/ln2 ≈ 4.5324 i` (|L₁₂|=5.4e-16 there), while its
   mirror slot is **empty** (|L₁₂(1−s̄)|=0.569) and the completed function is
   no longer self-reciprocal (\|Gauss sum\|²=4, not 12). This realizes
   RH-DIRECTION.md's Phase-3.2 falsification instinct with *actual*
   L-functions: **mirror symmetry is what keeps the spectrum on the line.**
   It is a theorem-level mechanism explanation, not a proof of RH.
