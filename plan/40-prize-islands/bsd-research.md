### D1. The statement of record (verbatim source of record)

Source of record: the CMI booklet "The Millennium Prize Problems"
(Clay Mathematics Institute, 2000; the Wiles chapter), as
hosted at claymath.org/library/monographs/MPPc.pdf (fetched
2026-08-24; the live claymath.org BSD page carries only the
audience-introduction — the formal statement lives in the
booklet). Verbatim (booklet, p. 32):

> "Conjecture (Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer). The Taylor
> expansion of L(C, s) at s = 1 has the form L(C, s) =
> c(s − 1)^r + higher order terms with c ≠ 0 and r =
> rank(C(Q)). In particular this conjecture asserts that
> L(C, 1) = 0 ⇔ C(Q) is infinite."

The booklet's own setup (p. 32) is EXACTLY the machinery bands
1–2 built on the line: C : y² = x³ + ax + b with a, b ∈ Z;
N_p := #{solutions of y² ≡ x³ + ax + b (mod p)}; a_p := p −
N_p; and the L-series defined as the product over p ∤ 2Δ of
(1 − a_p p^(−s) + p^(1−2s))^(−1) — "incomplete because we omit
the Euler factors for primes p | 2Δ" — "converge for Re(s) >
3/2", with the holomorphic continuation "now proved"
(modularity). The refined version (booklet p. 32, Remark 1):
"one has to define Euler factors at primes p | 2Δ to obtain the
completed L-series, L*(C, s). The conjecture then predicts that
L*(C, s) ∼ c* (s − 1)^r ...".

**This is the bridge the research record owes the store:** the
"uniform family" S15 built on the line IS the booklet's
incomplete series (the panel holds over the primes not dividing
the conductor), and the "record family" IS the booklet's
completed series (the one-term local factor at the ramified
primes — the corrections 4/5 and 1406/1407 are precisely the
omitted Euler factors at the single ramified prime of each
panel). The statement is about the COMPLETED L-series at s = 1;
the correction layer S15 held is what makes the statement's
object the record family, not the uniform one. (Panel note: the
booklet factors in the UNIFORM form at unramified primes — the
exact S14 a_p census; no conflict with the panel's
general-Weierstrass shape for B: booklet's a_p convention
p − N_p applied to B's own point counts, as S14 did.)
